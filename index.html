Photmetry

Aperture photometry is a technique used in astronomy to measure the brightness (flux) of celestial objects, such as stars or galaxies, by summing the pixel values within a defined geometric region, typically a circle or ellipse, centered on the object.Every pixel in the aperture contains both source photons and sky photons. Sky photons must be estimated and subtracted. The standard procedure is to measure the sky level in a circular annulus centered on the source, with inner radius r(in) and outer radius r(out), where r (in) > r(apperture) by a comfortable margin of at least 2–3 pixels. The sky per pixel S(sky) is estimated from the statistics of the annulus pixels.
